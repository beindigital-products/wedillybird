Prepared 2026-10-18 for 30 TikTok videos (75-100 s, English, US/UK, voice-over). Season: autumn to winter 2026/27, with many viewers planning 2027 weddings and engagement season (holidays) approaching.

## 0. How to read this bank

**Item fields:** id, topic, core insight, supporting points (3-5), example or script line, myth vs truth, suggested hook (8-12 words, word count in brackets), best-fit video format (list / myth-buster / script / timeline / story), ease of visualising (easy / hard), sources (publisher, title, date, URL), confidence.

**Confidence scale**
- **high** = read on the publisher's own page (or primary government/statute/data page) in this research run, and consistent with at least one other source or with itself.
- **medium** = single source, or read only through a search-tool page extract (see access log), or sources disagree, or the point is practitioner advice rather than data.
- **low** = extracted figure that could not be re-checked; do not quote a number from a low item without opening the original.

**Tags**
- `no-number` = the item is written without figures on purpose (no reputable sourced figure was found, or only vendor marketing figures exist).
- `sources-disagree` = reputable sources give different answers; the item says so. Never state one number as "the rule".
- `jurisdiction` = rules differ by state/country; always name the jurisdiction on screen.
- `tie-in` = natural, non-pushy bridge to WhatsApp invitations / RSVP tracking / QR check-in / shared gallery (see section 6).
- `computed` = arithmetic done by the researcher from sourced inputs (shown in the item). Not a quote.
- `suggested wording` = script lines written by the researcher as examples. They are NOT quotes from anyone and carry no statistics.

**Dates:** "updated" = date shown on the page ("Last updated"). Several publishers show only an update date. Undated pages are marked "undated".

## 1. ITEMS

### A. JUST ENGAGED (T01-T06)

### T01 · The first 10 things to do after getting engaged
- **Core insight:** The best first-month plan is mostly "don't rush": celebrate, tell people in the right order, then agree a date window and budget before you book anything.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. Celebrate first, phone away, somewhere meaningful (Zola's step 1).
  2. Tell your closest people in person or by phone before you post; save text announcements for people you are less close to.
  3. Shortlist date windows: season or month, check holidays/birthdays/conflicts, ask key people about availability, then narrow to a few dates to ask venues about.
  4. Draft a budget and timeline together. Zola says there is no required timeline; the usual order is location + date, contact venues, book one, then other vendors (planner, caterer, entertainment).
  5. Start a running list of who sends cards or gifts (for thank-you notes) and agree what you both want before booking anything; hold off on the wedding party, attire and guest invitations (T02); size and insure the ring (T04); expect unsolicited advice and keep only what helps. Optional extras: engagement party (T03) and engagement shoot (often included free in a photography package).
- **Example / script:** *suggested wording* — "We're engaged! We wanted you to hear it from us first. No date yet, we're just soaking it in." *Ten-step cut for the voice-over (from the points above):* 1 celebrate; 2 tell your inner circle personally; 3 post when ready; 4 shortlist date windows; 5 draft budget and timeline; 6 venue first, then other vendors; 7 start the thank-you list; 8 hold off on wedding party, attire and invitations; 9 size and insure the ring; 10 expect advice, keep what helps.
- **Myth vs truth:** Myth: you must lock a date and venue within weeks. Truth: Zola's own guide starts with "celebrate" and states there is no required timeline; average engagements run 15-18 months (T05).
- **Hook (10 words):** "Just got engaged? Do these ten things before booking anything."
- **Format:** list | **Visualise:** easy
- **Sources:** Zola (McCall Minnor), "Just Engaged? Here's What To Do Next!", updated 2025-10-13 — https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/to-dos-after-you-get-engaged
- **Confidence:** high

### T02 · Three things not to commit to in the first month
- **Core insight:** Asking the wedding party, buying attire and informally inviting people are the commitments that are hardest to undo, so wait until budget, venue and style are settled.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. Zola: wait a few weeks before asking anyone to be in the wedding; first settle budget, venue and wedding style because they decide how many people you can include.
  2. Keep guest talk vague and noncommittal "to avoid awkwardness if someone isn't on the final list".
  3. Do not try on or buy dresses/suits until you know your budget and what you want; rushed purchases can lead to regret or financial strain.
  4. Early research is normal: in Zola's 2026 First Look Report (11,500+ couples) 19% entered full planning mode before getting engaged, 10% toured venues and 7% booked a venue before the proposal.
- **Example / script:** *suggested wording* — "We're not inviting anyone yet while we sort budget and venue, but we love that you're excited with us."
- **Myth vs truth:** Common impulse: ask the bridesmaids the next day. Zola's advice: wait a few weeks.
- **Hook (11 words):** "Three things not to commit to in your first month engaged."
- **Format:** list | **Visualise:** easy
- **Sources:** Zola, "Just Engaged? Here's What To Do Next!", updated 2025-10-13 — https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/to-dos-after-you-get-engaged · Zola, "2026 Wedding Trends: Zola's First Look Report" (11,500+ couples), updated 2026-10-16 — https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/the-first-look-report
- **Confidence:** high

### T03 · Your engagement-party list is secretly your wedding list
- **Core insight:** Zola's rule: anyone invited to the engagement party should also be invited to the wedding, so the party is your first guest list.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. Two formats: a surprise party before you announce, or a more formal party a few weeks after the engagement.
  2. Exception Zola itself gives: if you are going kid-free, you can invite children to the engagement party or rehearsal dinner instead of the wedding.
  3. Engagement shoots are often included free with a photography package; the photos can be used for save-the-dates, the wedding website or invitations.
- **Example / script:** *suggested wording* — "It's a small family dinner, so everyone there is definitely on the wedding list."
- **Myth vs truth:** Myth: an engagement party is "just a party". Truth: Zola treats invitees as future wedding guests (kids are its stated exception).
- **Hook (11 words):** "Your engagement party guest list is secretly your wedding guest list."
- **Format:** myth-buster | **Visualise:** easy
- **Sources:** Zola, "Just Engaged? Here's What To Do Next!", updated 2025-10-13 — https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/to-dos-after-you-get-engaged · Zola (McCall Minnor), "How to Stick to a Small Wedding Guest List", updated 2025-10-13 — https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/small-wedding-guest-list
- **Confidence:** high (one publisher; consistent across two Zola pages)

### T04 · Ring: size it, appraise it, insure it separately
- **Core insight:** Zola advises sizing the ring immediately if it does not fit and insuring it with a jewelry-insurance provider (after an appraisal) rather than only adding it to a homeowner's or renter's policy.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. Get an appraisal from a jeweler or certified professional before you insure.
  2. Resizing: minor adjustments under $50, major ones can reach the hundreds; some jewelers resize free, so ask at purchase (Here Comes The Guide, updated 2026-08-08).
  3. Value at risk, for context: Zola's cost index lists the average engagement ring at $5,200 and wedding rings at $1,500.
- **Example / script:** *suggested order* — appraisal, then insurance quote, then resizing.
- **Myth vs truth:** n/a
- **Hook (10 words):** "Insure the ring this week, before you do anything else."
- **Format:** list | **Visualise:** easy
- **Sources:** Zola, "Just Engaged? - **Confidence:** medium (insurance advice is single-source; resizing figures are a wedding-blog estimate)

### T05 · How long is the average engagement? Depends who you ask

### B. PLANNING TIMELINE (T07-T10)

### T07 · Twelve months to one month in one view
- **Core insight:** Zola's 2026 checklist puts the big bookings in months 11-8 and the paperwork and follow-up in months 7-1.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. 12+ months: budget, rough guest list, planner decision, start venue research. Month 11: book venue, choose wedding party, engagement photos.
  2. Month 10: photographer (share a shot list), officiant, attire appointments, registry. Month 9: caterer tastings, florist, hotel room block.
  3. Month 8: send save-the-dates (6-8 months out, earlier for destination) and book the band/DJ. Month 7: review contracts and payment schedules; research name-change rules.
  4. Month 6: research your state/county marriage-license rules (deadlines, fees, waiting periods). Month 5: weather plan B. Month 4: order invitations.
  5. Month 2: mail invitations no later than six weeks out and start collecting RSVPs. Month 1: chase RSVPs, get the license, finalize seating, send the day-of timeline to vendors.
- **Example / script:** "Month 9 is when you book the florist AND the hotel block."
- **Myth vs truth:** Myth: invitations go out 3 months before. Truth: Zola's checklist says no later than six weeks; Zola's separate invitation article says 3-4 months (see T29, `sources-disagree`).
- **Hook (11 words):** "Your whole wedding timeline, twelve months to one, in sixty seconds."
- **Format:** timeline | **Visualise:** easy
- **Sources:** Zola (Francine Crociata), "Your Ultimate Wedding Planning Checklist", updated 2026-05-14 — https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/your-ultimate-wedding-planning-checklist
- **Confidence:** high (flag the internal inconsistency on save-the-date and invitation timing)

### T08 · Book the venue first: why the order matters
- **Core insight:** Venue, then photographer, then caterer/florist, then band/DJ: each booking narrows the next, and some venues restrict which vendors you may use.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. Zola's timeline: venue at 11 months, photographer at 10, caterer tastings and florist at 9, band/DJ at 8.
  2. Zola's engagement guide: once you have a location and date, contact venues, book one, then look at the planner, caterer and entertainment.
  3. WeddingWire's venue checklist (Q11): some venues keep a preferred-vendor list or restrict florists, musicians and planners; ask if exceptions are possible.
  4. Here Comes The Guide: some venues charge a fee to bring in outside vendors or caterers.
- **Example / script:** "Can we hire any photographer, or only your preferred list? Is there an outside-vendor fee?" (adapted from WeddingWire Q11 and Here Comes The Guide)
- **Myth vs truth:** Myth: you can hire any vendor anywhere. Truth: restrictions and fees are common; ask before you book the venue.
- **Hook (9 words):** "Book your venue first. Here's why the order matters."
- **Format:** list | **Visualise:** easy
- **Sources:** Zola checklist, updated 2026-05-14 — https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/your-ultimate-wedding-planning-checklist · WeddingWire (Kim Forrest), "The 24 Questions to Ask a Wedding Venue BEFORE Booking", 2021-05-04 — https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-ideas/recently-engaged/12-questions-to-ask-a-wedding-venue · Here Comes The Guide, updated 2026-08-08 — https://www.herecomestheguide.com/wedding-ideas/hidden-wedding-costs
- **Confidence:** high

### T09 · Months six to four: the admin window
- **Core insight:** Months 6-4 are when the boring, expensive admin lands: license rules, weather plan B, and the invitation order.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. Month 6: research marriage-license requirements (deadlines, fees, waiting periods) and walk the venue with your vendors.
  2. Month 5: build a weather plan B for outdoor events, finalize attire including alterations, and scout the rehearsal-dinner venue (Zola suggests most guests be within a 45-minute drive).
  3. Month 4: order invitations, one per household, and order 10-15% extra envelopes; arrange transport; order signage.
  4. Price anchors from Zola's 2026 cost index: invitation suite about $500 (range $400-600); alterations about $550 (range $300-800).
- **Example / script:** `computed` — 80 households means about 88-92 envelopes.
- **Myth vs truth:** Myth: one invitation per guest. Truth: Zola says one per household.
- **Hook (11 words):** "Months six to four: the boring tasks that save your wedding."

- **Confidence:** high

### T10 · The last month: five things that cannot slip
- **Core insight:** The final month is mostly follow-up: chase RSVPs, get the license, lock seating, send the timeline to vendors, and prepare labeled tip envelopes.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. Follow up on RSVPs one to two weeks after the deadline, then finalize the seating chart.
  2. Get the marriage license and finalize the day-of timeline, including vendor arrival times; Joy says share the timeline with vendors at least two weeks before.
  3. Set aside cash for vendor tips, labeled by recipient (Zola tipping guide: labeled envelopes, one trusted person to hand them out).
  4. One week out: final payments, final details to the caterer, emergency kit, last call with the planner. Day before: rehearse, confirm delivery times and locations.
- **Example / script:** "Label every tip envelope with the vendor's name and the amount, and give the list to one trusted person."
- **Myth vs truth:** n/a

### T40 · Who sits where: seating rules planners follow
- **Core insight:** Divorced parents get separate tables with their own supporters; skip the singles table; seat older guests near exits and restrooms and away from speakers.
- **Supporting points:**

### T55 · iPhone shared albums have limits that matter for weddings
- **Core insight:** Apple's Shared Albums created in iOS 26 or earlier cap at 5,000 items, 100 subscribers and shrink photos to 2,048 px; albums created in version 27 or later have no fixed item limit but count against the owner's iCloud storage and cap at 100 participants.
- **Supporting points:**
  1. Version 26 or earlier: items stay in iCloud without counting toward the owner's storage; up to 100 subscribers and 5,000 combined photos and videos per album; shared photos are reduced to 2,048 px on the long edge; videos up to 15 minutes at up to 720p; a contributor can add 1,000 items per hour and 10,000 per day.
  2. Version 27 or later: up to 100 participants including the owner; no fixed limit on photos and videos, only the owner's iCloud storage; items are stored at full resolution in their original formats and count toward the owner's storage (contributors' items too).

### T86 · Older guests at your wedding: seating, rest, rides and food

## 3. FIFTEEN ETIQUETTE QUESTIONS THAT SPARK COMMENTS

Each question is phrased as comment bait; the "authority says" line is what the sources actually say, so the voice-over can give a sourced answer or invite debate. Use one line, one source, no certainty beyond that.

1. **Do you have to invite a guest's live-in partner you've never met?** Emily Post (Anna Post): spouses, fiancé(e)s and live-in partners must be invited; casual dating partners need not be. (T23)
2. **Is "no ring, no bring" dead?** WeddingWire (2020): outdated; decide case by case. (T23)
3. **Should "adults only" go on the invitation?** Emily Post: no, let the envelope names signal it. Zola's 2026 wording guide offers "Adults only, please". Sources disagree. (T24)
4. **Is it rude to text a wedding invitation?** Etiquette specialist Anne Chertoff: not unless everyone is getting a text. (T36)
5. **Are email save-the-dates fine?** Emily Post: yes; printed invitations preferred. (T35)
6. **Can the RSVP be online-only?** Emily Post: email reply is acceptable if it is offered as an option. (T35)
7. **Thank-you notes: three months or a year?** Emily Post: three months, the year is a myth. Zola: three months, up to a year acceptable. (T64)
8. **Do guests have a year to send a wedding gift?** Emily Post: "another myth"; at the latest within three months of the wedding. (T65)
9. **Does a gift have to cover your plate?** Emily Post: "simply untrue"; no rule. (T66)
10. **Is a B-list rude?** Most planners advise against it (Philadelphia Magazine), Chertoff says it works if RSVPs show room and timing is early; Zola says make sure it isn't obvious. (T22)
11. **Should every wedding-party member get a plus-one?** Zola: "pretty common"; planner Mindy Weiss: yes unless money is very tight. (T68)
12. **Do parents who pay get to add guests?** Zola: some license for input, but "this is not their party". (T26)
13. **Is asking for cash instead of gifts okay?** Emily Post: cash or check is acceptable and the registry is a convenience, not a mandate. (T67)
14. **Is it okay to chase non-responders in the group chat?** The Knot: avoid group chats and public tags; message individually, then call. (T31)
15. **Do you owe an invitation to someone just because you attended their wedding?** Zola: no, don't invite someone just for that. (T27)

*Other strong prompts (not counted):* service charge versus tip (T14); who gets a "plus-one" on the wedding party; whether the engagement-party list equals the wedding list (T03).
