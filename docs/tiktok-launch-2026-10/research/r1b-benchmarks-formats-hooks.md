## 1. Eleven things the research says (executive summary)

1. **Even the biggest wedding brands are small on TikTok.** The Knot 151K, WeddingWire ≈412K, Zola ≈65K, Minted 19.7K, Bridebook 22K; Joy 1,783, Paperless Post ≈2.7K, RSVPify 26, Weddybird 125. Creators and planners (Claire 1.2M, Finance Brides 536K, Vegas Wedding Planner ≈456K, Plus One Planning ≈429K) hold the attention.
2. **Brand tool-explainers draw tens to hundreds of likes; problem-first creator posts draw thousands to tens of thousands** (snapshots: Joy 228–669, Zola "Why Zola?" 264, The Knot website explainer 15; Laur's etiquette video 17.3K, Finance Brides budget tip ≈42.9K, a creator's WeddingWire seating-chart video 141.5K). Lead with the problem, let the product be a prop.
3. **RSVP tooling is an empty lane on TikTok.** RSVPify is dormant, Paperless Post and Greenvelope are near-absent, Joy is tiny. RSVP *method* tips exist (QR RSVP tip 3,118 likes; RSVP-cadence posts) but come from planners and stationers, not tool brands. Day-of operations appears in planner BTS and timeline tips; no tool-led content was found.
4. **QR photo albums are crowded with vendors** (e.g. `@eventoly.com` explainers ≈3,000–3,800 likes each). Do not lead with the gallery; use it as the closing beat (memories after the party).
5. **WeddingTok is anxious.** A Canadian long-read (Oct 2025) says #WeddingTok makes viewers feel "behind schedule" [Tyee-25]. Zola's #ZolaFckItBucket and The Knot's "minus the overwhelm" both position against pressure. A calm "you are not behind" voice is a real differentiator and matches your brand voice.
6. **Mature planner accounts have plateaued.** Between Sept 2024 and Oct 2026 Claire stayed at 1.2M and Finance Brides slipped from 562.6K to 536.3K, while Plan With Laur rose from 51.8K to 73.8K (§2.6). Growth has to come from format and series differentiation, not from a rising tide.
7. **Serial and "Part N" structure is proven in this niche** (Vegas Wedding Planner's multi-part BTS; Sistered States says to label parts; ReesaTeesa's 50-part series passed 500M views — a non-wedding precedent).
8. **No AI-host wedding account surfaced.** No notable public wedding account built on an AI-generated host was found in 2026 listings. The lane is open, but so is the trust risk: label it loudly (§6.1).
9. **"WhatsApp-first" needs a US translation.** Your English landing copy says guests in the US and Canada get an SMS and WhatsApp is used "everywhere else"; Pew (Nov 2025) puts WhatsApp at 32% of US adults vs 90% of UK online adults (Ofcom 2025). Lead US videos with "by text", mention WhatsApp for international guests; lead UK / AU videos with WhatsApp (§5.2).
10. **Name risk is real but not on TikTok yet.** Weddybird has 125 TikTok followers and a German bio, so TikTok confusion is small today. The risk is cross-platform search (your own audit shows the brand query "wedillybird" already returns Weddybird) and a future trademark conflict (§5.3).
11. **Claims that must stay out of the videos** (found in your own English copy): "98% open rate", "RSVP in 30 seconds", "tested on 3G with a 2-second load", any price, any testimonial. They are unverified performance claims under your own claims-and-proof rules (§3.4).

## 3. Twenty repeatable formats for 75–100 s

### 3.0 Universal skeleton and retention ladder (applies to all 20)

A 75–100 s voice-over at ≈150 words per minute is **≈190–250 words**. Secondary 2026 length guides disagree on the ideal length and agree only that longer videos trade completion for total watch time and must hold attention [Len-secondary]; so every format carries a built-in retention device.

| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | **Hook** (≤12 words) | Same words as the first-frame caption. Topic keyword in the first three words (searchability) |
| 4–10 s | **Promise + stake** | Who it is for, what they walk away with, an open loop ("number four changes everything after it") |
| 10–70 s | **Proof, then steps: 3–5 beats × 12–18 s** | Proof = something visible that makes the promise credible (an example, a rule, a real screen with demo data), not a statistic. Each beat ends in a micro-payoff; visual change every 2.5–4 s |
| ~35–45 s | **Mid re-hook** | "Here's the part people skip." (no superlatives, no stats) |
| 70–88 s | **Payoff** | The answer, the script, the summary card — at ≈75–85% of the runtime, not the last second |
| 88–100 s | **CTA** | One ask only: follow for part N / save / "see a sample invitation (link in bio)". Never urgency |
| last 5 s | **Bonus line** | A small extra for viewers who stayed; echo the hook to invite a rewatch |

Caption: topic keyword first, then 3–5 tags including a year-bride tag (#2027bride, #2028bride — seen in Finance Brides and Claire captions) and `#wedillybird`, never `#weddybird`.

Faceless production notes: Norah is never shown facing the camera (hands, back, side, desk, notebook); decor B-roll in the light "editorial wedding" look (ivory, blush, champagne, sage; gold only as ornament); product beats are real screen captures with demo data labelled **"Demo data"**; burned-in captions ≤6 words per line inside TikTok's safe zone.

### 3.1 The twenty formats

Legend: **Aud** = Couples / Planners / Both. **Signal** = market evidence (or "extrapolated" when none was found). Hook IDs refer to the hook bank in §4. Every block follows hook → promise → proof → steps → payoff → CTA.

**F1 · The Pre-Send List** — "Five things to settle before you send invitations" · Aud: Couples · Signal: numbered lists and "things I've done / won't do" dominate WeddingTok [Tyee-25]; Sistered States' "planning 101" series [SS-2020]
- **Beats:** **Hook** H01 → **Promise** "five decisions, in order; number four changes everything after it" → **Proof** 4-s montage of one guest answering in three places → **Steps** 5 × ≈12 s (decision → why → action) → **Payoff** order-of-operations card → **CTA** "save for invitation week; part 2 = after you send".
- **Holds past 60 s:** ranked open loop, each decision a mini-story, part-2 tease.
- **Hooks:** H01 "Five things to settle before you send a single invitation." · H02 "Three invitation details you'll regret leaving until later." · H03 "Don't design the invitation first. Start with this instead."
- **Tie-in:** decision "where do replies go?" → ≤10 s capture of the RSVP page and live counter; "who gets which link" → one unique link per guest.

**F2 · Red Flag Radar** · Aud: Both · Signal: vendor red-flag and "bad news" openers (Finance Brides, Claire)
- **Beats:** **Hook** H04 → **Promise** "how to spot it and what to do instead" → **Proof** an invented "before" screen: replies in several channels, no single list → **Steps** 4 red flags (sign → why it bites → fix) → **Payoff** the green-flag version of the same setup → **CTA** "send this to your partner or co-planner".
- **Holds:** red-vs-green contrast; flag #3 "sounds harmless" delays the reveal; it ends on the fix, not on fear.
- **Hooks:** H04 "Four red flags your RSVP process is already in trouble." · H05 "Red flags on a guest list that become seating drama." · H06 "This one sentence from a venue should make you pause."
- **Tie-in:** red flag "replies in five places" → one consolidated list; "no idea who is pending" → pending filter; red flag "photos scattered across guests' phones" → shared gallery (one line). Calm voice, no scare tactics.

### 3.3 Product tie-ins: natural homes and how to avoid the ad voice

| Feature | Natural homes | Keep out of |
|---|---|---|
| Text / WhatsApp invitations (one link per guest, cinematic opening) | F5, F6, F8, F14, F15, F17, F19 | Hooks (lead with the problem) |
| RSVP tracking (live counter, pending list, reminders, dietary summary, CSV) | F1, F2, F4, F7, F10, F11, F13, F16 | — |
| QR check-in (offline) | F10 (Entries 07–08), F12, F16, F20 | F3, F6 |
| Shared photo gallery | F10 (Entries 09–10), F17 (closing line), F2 (red flag: scattered photos), F18 (post-event item) | Early hooks; the QR-album niche is crowded |
| Planner dashboard / branded portal | F9, F12, F13, F16, F20; Series 3 | Couple-facing videos |

Rules:
1. **Solve first, show second.** The problem is solved in tool-agnostic terms before any product appears.
2. **One product beat per video, ≤15 s on screen, the name spoken at most twice** (one natural mention + end card). About one video in three needs a product beat at all.
3. **Narrate the screen, do not sell the feature:** "here's the list of who hasn't answered", not "our powerful dashboard".
4. **Admit limits** ("guests without phones need paper").
5. **One CTA:** "See a sample invitation" → the public demo. The repo has a `/demo` route (English at `/en/demo`, listed in the sitemap); the Sept 2026 audit describes it as a real invitation with a fictional couple. Confirm it is deployed before the first post.
6. **Always label demo data** and never show real guest or client data (your own approval checklist).
7. **Safe product vocabulary** (from the English landing copy): one unique link per guest, invitation page in your colours, no app for guests, live confirmed / declined / pending counter, dietary needs aggregated, CSV export for the caterer, offline QR check-in, moderated shared gallery, PDF album.

### 3.4 Claims to keep out of the videos (flagged against your claims-and-proof rules)

- "**98% open rate**" and "22% for email" — present in `messages/en.json` (landing channels + FAQ) with no source. The September audit lists the French "98 % des invitations ouvertes" among invented numbers.
- "**RSVP in 30 seconds**" (landing copy) — a performance claim; show the real taps instead.
- "**Tested on 3G, 2-second load / under 80 KB**" — needs measurement proof.
- Any price (human review required per `offers.md`), any "couples love it", rating, testimonial, usage count, or named-competitor comparison.
- "Older guests will manage" — say "no app, no account" instead.

## 6. Three recurring series

### 6.1 Series 1 — Norah's Notebook (lightly fictional diary / serial)

- **Promise:** "A wedding told one entry at a time — invented people, familiar problems, a practical fix in every entry."
- **Repeatable intro line (≈5 s, same every time):** "Norah's Notebook, entry [number]. The story is fiction. The fix is real."
- **Visual identity cue:** ivory ruled notebook page with paper grain, blush ribbon bookmark, a gold ✦ divider, the entry number in large Bodoni italic. Norah appears only as hands, back or side at one oak desk with a sage notebook; never facing the camera. A pen **crosses out the solved problem and writes the next one in blush ink** on the end card (the cliffhanger device). A small sage tag **FICTION · AI CHARACTER** sits lower-left for the first 3 s and on the end card.
- **Cadence suggestion:** one entry roughly every 1–2 days (10 entries fit the 15-day window); extend only if retention is strong. Each entry must work for a new viewer on its own.

**Season 1 arc (story weeks before the wedding; couple invented: "Mara & Eli")**

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 04 | The Plus-One (6) | Plus-one policy and wording | Named-guest handling | A name nobody invited |

Entry hooks (≤12 words): 01 "Entry 01. Four tabs, three versions, one guest list." · 02 "Entry 02. Replies are arriving in four different places." · 03 "Entry 03. A 'maybe' arrived with a heart emoji." · 04 "Entry 04. Someone replied for six people. We invited four." · 05 "Entry 05. Keep entries 01–02 tool-agnostic (spreadsheet and group-chat chaos) so the series earns trust before any product appears.

**How to keep the fiction unmistakably fiction (never a testimonial, never a planner's experience claim)**

1. **Label at four layers:** a burned-in tag (first 3 s + end card); the spoken intro line; the first caption line ("Fiction: Norah and the couple are invented. The tips are real."); and a pinned comment with the same sentence. Burned-in text survives reposts and stitches; platform labels may not.
2. **Switch TikTok's AI-generated-content label on for every entry** (realistic AI person or voice) and keep any C2PA metadata the generation tool adds. Secondary summaries (e.g. CineRads, 21 Jul 2026) say realistic AI humans are the main trigger for the label, that purpose-built brand avatars are acceptable, and that presenting a synthetic person as a named *real customer* is treated as deceptive. Verify in the app and Help Center; the official page did not render.
3. **Norah never claims experience.** No "I've planned N weddings", "my clients", "in my years…". She is a character learning inside a story, not a planner.
4. **No testimonial structure.** Norah never reviews the product ("Wedillybird saved me hours"). On screen, the product is a neutral tool described in the present tense. If the product is ever recommended, the *brand* speaks ("At Wedillybird we built…"), not the fictional character. The FTC's fake-reviews rule (16 CFR Part 465, announced 14 Aug 2024) prohibits fake or false testimonials, including AI-generated ones and ones from people who do not exist or lacked real experience [FTC-24]; keeping Norah out of the "customer" role is the safeguard.
5. **No outcome claims.** Story outcomes are framed "in this story". Add to the end card: "Demo data. Results depend on your guests."
6. **Nothing real is borrowed:** invented names, no real vendors, venues, logos or messages; no real-looking reviews or screenshots of conversations.
7. **Norah stays out of the comments.** Replies come from the brand voice ("Norah is a fictional AI host; the team replies"). No in-character answers, which would blur the line.
8. **Stand-alone value:** each entry teaches a general, checkable tip, so it is education in a story wrapper rather than an ad in disguise.
9. **No fake urgency in cliffhangers:** story tension only ("the Wi-Fi died"), never "only 3 days left".
10. **A "how this is made" pinned video (15–20 s)** states that Norah is an AI character, scripts are written by the Wedillybird team, and the product screens are real with demo data.
11. **Legal and regulatory re-check before launch:** TikTok's current AIGC rules; the FTC Endorsement Guides (virtual-endorser language not verified here); and EU AI Act Art. 50 transparency duties that secondary sources say apply from 2 Aug 2026 (relevant to a French team and EU viewers) — confirm with counsel.
